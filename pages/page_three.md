# Open Source Software Orange in European Studies 

## Orange Data Mining

Orange Data Mining is a software used to analyse large sets of data files and find results in thousands of data points and texts. It helps to mine the data by different criteria, such as speaker, date, country or other parameters. With its Text add-on, it is also helpful for finding patterns in speeches, for example the most frequent words or main topics.

Orange was made by a team of researchers at the Faculty of Computer and Information Science, University of Ljubljana, in Slovenia. The project started in the late 1990s, and today it is developed by the university's Bioinformatics Laboratory. The main developers are Janez Demšar and Blaž Zupan ([Demšar et al., 2013](https://jmlr.org/papers/v14/demsar13a.html)).

## Orange in European Studies

In European Studies, we often work with many speeches on the same topic. Orange helps us find patterns in the speeches of different speakers from different regions and roles. Speeches can also be sorted by date, so we can see how the views of different speakers change over time. Recently, I worked on the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and we identified how European leaders responded in different phases of the war, from February 2022 to 2026.

## The five checks

- **Licence:** [GPL-3.0](https://github.com/biolab/orange3/blob/master/LICENSE). I can use, study, change and share Orange, but if I share a changed version, it must stay open source.
- **Last change:** 13 November 2026.
- **People:** 110 contributors. The top contributor is Janez Demšar.
- **Questions:** 102 issues are open and 2,504 are closed.
- **Money:** Orange is developed by the Bioinformatics Laboratory at the University of Ljubljana. Users can support the lab through GitHub Sponsors and donations.

## My verdict

Yes, I would use this software in my thesis. My thesis is on EU–US relations, and I will analyse a lot of data, including speeches, news articles and documents from the European Commission. I will use Orange to analyse this large amount of text. I believe it because it is actively developed (the latest release came out in November 2026), has 110 contributors and is backed by a university, so it does not depend on one person. I could also leave it if I had to. Orange can export my files and  data to file formats like CSV and Excel, so I could continue my work in another tool.
